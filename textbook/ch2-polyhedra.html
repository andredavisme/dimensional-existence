The Platonic Solids

There are exactly five regular convex polyhedra in 3D space — the Platonic Solids. These are the only shapes where every face, edge, and vertex is identical. They form the physical upper bound of symmetric finite-state encoders.

  • Tetrahedron (d4) — 4 faces
  • Cube (d6) — 6 faces
  • Octahedron (d8) — 8 faces
  • Dodecahedron (d12) — 12 faces
  • Icosahedron (d20) — 20 faces

Gravity as the Constraint

When a polyhedron is rolled on a flat surface, gravity collapses all possible face states into exactly one outcome. This is the physical analog of a database constraint: infinite pre-roll possibility reduced to a single valid row state.
